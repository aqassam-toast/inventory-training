FAQs

Quick answers to the most frequently asked questions.

Test Kitchen

For restaurant-focused customers exploring Toast Inventory through the Test Kitchen beta program. You are looking for an all-in-one POS+Inventory solution that helps you keep stock of ingredients that are linked to menu item sales through recipes.

Getting Started

I'm already using a Toast inventory product. What happens to it?

Toast currently offers three inventory products:

  • xtraChef
  • International Inventory
  • Retail Inventory

Customers on all three products will eventually migrate to Toast Inventory. The transition will be least disruptive for retail customers — Toast Inventory is a real-time perpetual inventory solution with a user experience that closely mirrors what retail customers use today. The underlying engine is being overhauled to support an AI-first inventory future with the flexibility to handle a wide range of inventory use cases, but day-to-day workflows will feel familiar.

What each path looks like:

  • Toast Retail customers will be upgraded in place, since the product is already built on top of Point of Sale. This is the most seamless path, and timing depends on which features you use most.
  • International Inventory customers will be migrated to Toast Inventory.
  • xtraChef customers will be able to opt in. You'll see a prompt in xtraChef offering the migration, along with a period where you don't pay for the new product. Purchase data, product setup, inventory, and recipes all come across so you don't have to redo that work. This is targeted for late 2026.

You will not be forced off your current product before Toast Inventory is proven. If you're actively using a legacy product and it's working for you, end of life may be as far out as 2028.

Migration tooling available today:

  • Item and ingredient import is live now. If you have an inventory list from another system — including an xtraChef export — you can import it to get your data in and start testing.
  • Recipe import is targeted for the end of August 2026. It's being built against the xtraChef recipe export format first, then extended to other systems such as Margin Edge and Restaurant365. An example template will be posted here so you can review it before it ships.
  • Test Kitchen members will be the first group invited to test the automated xtraChef → Toast Inventory data transfer, by email.

In most cases your Toast onboarding consultant will run the import for you rather than you doing it yourself.

How do I get early access to Toast Inventory?

To get early access, follow these steps:

  • Sign up for Test Kitchen — you must be an active member and agree to the beta terms.
  • Watch the Test Kitchen Onboarding video — this walks you through what's available and how to get started.
  • Complete the access acknowledgement form — this registers your request and lets the team know which environment you'd like access to.

Once those are done, you'll receive a confirmation email when access has been granted — either to the shared sandbox or directly on your own production account, depending on what you request.

I have access, but I don't see any of my items. What's wrong?

This is almost always a configuration step that hasn't been completed yet — not a problem with your account.

Today you have to tell us which menus you want to manage in this ecosystem. Go to the Categories section and check the boxes for the menus you want to bring in. Once you do, your menu items will appear.

We try to complete this step for everyone when access is granted, so if items are missing it may simply have been missed. This requirement is going away very soon — all of your menu items will flow into the ecosystem automatically. Until then, it's an easy thing to trip over, so check Categories first.

If you've checked Categories and items still aren't appearing, reach out and we'll take a look together.

Do I need the Toast Retail module to use this?

No — you do not need to purchase the Toast Retail module to participate in the Test Kitchen.

Right now, accessing the features does require navigating to the Toast Retail section of the navigation. Starting August 16, 2026, a new navigation means you'll no longer have to click through Toast Retail to reach the new inventory product.

Is the navigation changing?

Yes, and fairly significantly. The navigation pane is being reorganized so there's a single, dedicated place to manage your items and your inventory.

Everyone in the Sandbox account will see the new navigation on August 16, 2026. Expect things to feel unfamiliar for a short period while you're seeing work in progress — that's the tradeoff of testing in the sandbox, where features land first.

Product & Functionality

Will inventory update automatically from invoices?

Yes. We're working toward a system where invoice ingestion updates inventory with minimal manual intervention, though mapping between purchases and products will still be necessary at the beginning.

Do recipes need to exist before sales? Can they be updated later?

The system is quite flexible. You'll be able to manually enter a plate cost that gets applied to sales to determine margin, even without recipes existing against those sellable items. This manual plate cost can be updated up to 30 days in the past.

Recipes are also flexible:

  • You can adjust recipes over time.
  • Changes are tracked so historical reporting remains intact — no need to create new menu items just because a recipe changes.
  • Retroactive recipe changes will be allowed, with restrictions: generally no more than 30 days back, and for complex historical edits (like recipe changes) you may be limited to 7 days.
Can multiple products share the same inventory?

Yes — this is a core use case (e.g., different pours from the same keg). The system is designed to support shared inventory across multiple sellable items.

What exists today: the Toast retail product has a limited version of inventory sharing. It has significant limitations and gaps, so treat it as a starting point rather than a complete solution. You can try it in the Sandbox account.

What's planned: a redesigned approach that is not yet built. Rather than routing everything through a recipe, you'll be able to declare that two items draw from common stock and give the ratio between them — for example, a can consumes one sixth of a pack, or a keg supports both pints and growlers. It handles fractions, so bottles, glass pours, and tastings of the same wine can all deplete from one pool. This removes the need to build a recipe just to link items that share stock.

Will batch production / prep workflows be supported?

Yes. Batch production will exist, but it will be manual at the start. We're figuring out what level of automation makes sense to keep batch production maintainable without a lot of human intervention.

Can I track inventory by sub-location (bar, kitchen, retail)?

Not fully in the first release, but this is a high-priority area of feedback and something we're actively investigating.

What types of items can I create?

When you add an item, you'll be able to choose from four types:

  • Retail
  • Prepared food and beverage — only this type gets the additional recipe configuration option
  • Ingredient
  • Supply

Bundles are coming soon. Bundles are short-lived SKUs — holiday bundles, gift sets — where you package several items together at a different price. Functionally it's similar to a recipe, but aimed at retail rather than restaurant businesses.

If you always create the same type of item, you can set a default so the selection prompt stops appearing. You can remove that default later from the edit view, and you can still create other item types through the secondary dropdown.

Do I manage items in Inventory or in Menu Manager?

Either — it's all one system. Toast Inventory and Menu Manager read from the same underlying data source, so work you do in one place shows up in the other. There is no mapping step and no duplication.

This is a deliberate departure from xtraChef, where mapping between systems was a constant source of work and errors. Here you do the work once, on the item you actually sell.

Practically, that means you can keep doing all your menu management, item modifications, and setup the traditional way, and only come into the inventory views when you want the granular pieces like recipes and stock tracking. Once you publish an item, it's available for sale at the price you configured and appears in Menu Manager and your menus interface as usual.

Does this support location-specific pricing?

Yes, in the calculations. If an item is $10 at one location and $12 at another, all margin and reporting calculations use the correct location's price. Nothing is lost.

What's missing is the ability to edit it in the new interface. This is a UI gap, not a data gap — continue setting location-based pricing where you do today and it will be honored.

We're adding pricing configurations to the new UI in this order:

  • Size pricing — soonest
  • Price levels — next
  • Location-specific and time-specific — last, as these are the most complex to represent
I'm multi-location (MLM). Do I have to recreate recipes at every location?

No. The recipe lives on the menu item itself, so it inherits whatever target and owner that menu item already has. If a menu item is targeted at 18 locations, the recipe applies at all 18.

You do not copy recipes, you do not re-add buttons per location, and you do not manage parallel copies. If you change the recipe, it changes everywhere the item is served.

This is a meaningful change from how many customers work today, and it may cause some growing pains — if the model creates a problem for your operation, we want to hear about it.

Which ordering channels deplete inventory?

All of them — Point of Sale, online ordering, kiosk, and catering & events. Depletion is real time, so a sale through any channel is reflected in stock on hand immediately.

You can also see which channel a given sale came through in the detailed activity view for an item.

Do I have to track stock on every item?

No, and this is one of the more useful aspects of the system. You choose which items to stock track and leave the rest alone. Plenty of customers care about margin and food cost without wanting to count everything.

One thing to know: if you count stock against an item during an inventory count, we'll automatically enable stock tracking for it and store that amount.

How is inventory cost calculated?

On a first-in, first-out (FIFO) basis. If you receive tomatoes at $2 each and later receive more at $2.50, the system consumes all of the $2 tomatoes before it starts consuming the more expensive ones.

This is fully traceable: you can follow cost of goods down to each individual sale and see which receive it drew from. Most of the time you won't need this level of detail, but it's there when a number doesn't look right and you want to verify the calculation yourself.

How do unit conversions work?

Conversions are stored on the ingredient itself. When you tell us that one each of something is equivalent to a pound, we store that on the item and apply it everywhere that ingredient is used, so you can review conversions in one place rather than hunting through recipes.

Unit conversions are the single most common source of error in theoretical inventory calculations. A mistyped conversion quietly corrupts actual-versus-theoretical numbers downstream, which is why we surface them for review. We're also working with ToastIQ to proactively flag conversions that look wrong.

Can I see who changed what, and when?

Yes. Audit tracking is built into the core of the product rather than bolted on. For a given item you can see who made a change, when they made it, and which surface it came from — for example a change made from the web item library versus one made on mobile.

This directly addresses the most common question we got in xtraChef: who changed what, when, and why is this number not what I expected? It won't be perfect at first — there may be user actions we haven't captured yet — so tell us if you find a gap.

Does waste tracking integrate with inventory?

Yes. Waste logged through the waste tracking module flows directly into the item's recent activity view, labeled as a POS waste adjustment, so you can account for waste alongside sales depletion.

You'll need to have waste reasons configured before you can log waste.

The goal is that damage, expiry, loss, and similar adjustments are the main reasons you come in to touch quantities manually — everything else should be handled by receiving and sales.

Can I control how many decimal places are used?

Not yet. The first version uses two decimal places for everything.

We've heard clearly that this isn't right for everyone — if you're counting whole units, extra decimal places make routine data entry slower and more error-prone. Configurable precision is under active consideration with the product team.

Can I edit items in bulk?

Yes. The item library supports both inline editing and bulk editing, and it covers merchandising and inventory fields together — including par min and par max — so you don't have to open individual item screens to make routine updates.

You can select many items at once and set values in bulk. Combined with custom attributes, this lets you slice your catalog however makes sense for your business and then edit the resulting set.

Inventory settings will not be added to the legacy Advanced Settings bulk management screen. The item library is intended to replace that need entirely. It's designed to work for someone brand new to inventory while still supporting power users making large numbers of changes quickly.

Can I add my own fields to items?

Yes — you can create custom attributes on items and use them however you need. One customer doing wholesale work on the side uses them to record the most recent lot number for an item.

Custom attributes can be printed on labels, and they're useful for slicing and filtering your catalog so you can make targeted bulk edits.

Can I add plating pictures or a recipe book for the kitchen?

Yes, in two stages:

  • End of August 2026 — images, GIFs, and video can be attached to recipes, so you can document what a finished plate should look like.
  • End of 2026 — integration with the Kitchen Display System, so your team can pull up a full recipe book on the KDS while prepping. Click into an item to see the plating image, and a short video where that's useful for your operation.

The KDS piece takes longer because it requires two very different platforms to communicate, so there's some plumbing to put in place first.

Automation & AI

Will the system automate reordering?

Eventually, yes — with alerts first, then recommendations, then optional automation. We expect this to evolve over time, not all at once.

Can I set par levels and trigger orders?

Partly today. Par-based workflows are supported for sellable items. Support for ingredients and supplies is coming soon.

You can set par min and par max values, including inline and in bulk from the item library.

Automated restock-to-par is not available yet. This is one of our most requested capabilities — for example, transferring a set quantity to a food truck every morning, or assuming a standard daily production run at a bakery. Today the plan is to handle this through ToastIQ automations, which is a power user flow. We're also exploring a simpler default rule so repeatable transfers don't require configuring an automation. If this is important to your operation, let us know — it's actively shaping the design.

Can the system automatically 86 items?

Yes — especially based on ingredient stock. More advanced logic (e.g., time-based replenishment) is being explored. We'll also offer capabilities for setting manual availability that is independent of stock levels.

Hardware & Integrations

Will there be integrations with scales or draft systems?

This is an active area of research. Barcode scanning is included, as well as barcode label generation and printing. We're exploring additional hardware integrations over time.

Does this integrate with accounting tools like QuickBooks?

Yes. Sales, payroll, and accounts payable invoice data all integrate with QuickBooks Online today in the US. For all other accounting systems, there is a CSV export available now.

Planned but not yet complete: Xero support for international markets, and country-specific QuickBooks Online implementations. We'll confirm timing as those get closer.

The last piece we're buttoning up is posting cost of goods from your inventory counts through to your accounting system. A dedicated webinar covering accounting integrations will follow once those pieces are in place.

Does this integrate with food distributors?

Yes. We're building EDI integration through a third party called Orderful, which is a different approach than xtraChef took.

The advantage is that we don't have to build and maintain each distributor connection ourselves. Once a supplier is integrated with Orderful, every customer benefits — including suppliers whose invoices have historically been painful to capture accurately because the receipts are hard to read.

Southern Glazer's is the initial rollout partner, with additional distributors to follow. The first partners are being onboarded now, targeted for the end of the quarter. As each one becomes available, we'll announce it by email to the Test Kitchen and post it here.

How do I get invoices into the system?

There are three ways, and you can mix them depending on the supplier:

  • Receive via email — your account gets a dedicated email address. Have suppliers send invoices there and they arrive automatically, with no manual step from you.
  • Mobile scan — scan an invoice on your phone and it's auto-scanned and received.
  • Web upload — upload an image of the invoice from the browser.

This is broadly similar to how xtraChef works today, with receive-via-email being the notable addition.

What can I do with labels and barcodes?

You can assign a barcode to any item — useful if you package something for grab-and-go and want it scannable. The system generates the barcode for you and lets you design and print labels.

Labels are fully customizable: choose which fields appear (including custom attributes), set the width and height for whatever label stock you use, and print individually or in bulk across many items. Most label printers are supported. This capability comes from building on top of the retail product.

Prep labels are not supported yet. There's more work needed before we'd recommend the tool for prep labeling specifically — that's targeted for the end of 2026.

Allergens & Dietary Tags

Can I track allergens? Can I customize the list?

Yes, you can tag items with allergens — but the allergen list itself is not customizable. Allergen labeling is legally regulated, so the list is fixed and maintained by a dedicated Toast team that stays on top of the regulations.

The list changes by country. If your account is configured for the US, Canada, or the UK, you'll see the allergens appropriate to that market's requirements.

Allergens will save and surface on your online ordering, Point of Sale, and third-party ordering systems — that last piping step is targeted for mid-August 2026.

What if I want to flag something that isn't an allergen?

Use dietary tags. Unlike allergens, dietary tags are fully customizable, because there aren't legal regulations governing them. They surface in the same places — online ordering and Point of Sale — so guests can filter and sort by them, but they're not presented as allergen information.

If there's something specific to your menu you want to call out or let guests filter on, this is the field to use.

Can allergens be set on ingredients and roll up to recipes?

Not yet, but this is actively being worked on and is targeted for the end of August 2026.

The goal is that you tag allergens once at the ingredient level and they automatically roll up to every recipe that uses that ingredient, rather than tagging each finished item by hand.

Reporting

What reporting does Toast Inventory include?

We took a different approach to reporting in this product than xtraChef did. There are three core reports, designed to bring sales and inventory data together in one place rather than giving you a large catalog of overlapping reports to navigate:

  • Sales & COGS
  • Inventory Snapshot
  • Inventory Roll Forward

A tutorial walking through all three reports is coming soon and will be posted in the Tutorials section.

Can I ask questions about my inventory data?

Yes — because Toast Inventory is built directly on top of the Point of Sale, you get the benefit of ToastIQ integration. You can ask questions in plain language instead of building a report, for example:

  • What are my stock levels across my locations?
  • Which items' margins have gone down more than 5% in the past six months?

This is also how we plan to surface data quality problems — for example, flagging unit conversions that look like they may have been entered incorrectly.

Advanced Use Cases

Can large catering orders be handled differently?

We haven't fully considered catering-specific operations yet, but a lot of great suggestions came up in the webinar. If a significant portion of your orders are catering-based, please reach out — we'd love to learn more about your use case.

What modifier functionality is coming in November?

Modifiers are configurations that can be set on sellable items at the time of checkout — for example, adding a topping, swapping a size, or removing an ingredient. Some of these modifications have inventory implications, where ingredients or packaging are added to or removed from the core sellable item's recipe.

In November, we're launching modifier support for recipes with three modifier types: Add, Remove, and Size.

  • Add and Remove modifiers: Click into a modifier to link it to an ingredient with a consumption quantity (unit conversions supported). When an order is placed with the modifier selected, the ingredient quantity is added to or removed from the core recipe and the plate cost updates accordingly.
  • Size modifiers: Each size gets its own recipe that builds on the core recipe. You can add or remove ingredients and packaging per size, and adjust quantities independently for each size — giving you full control over what goes into each variation.

Substitute modifiers will follow in early 2027.

When you configure a modifier recipe, we'll tell you how many menu items share that modifier. By default the recipe is shared across all of them, so changing it in one place changes it everywhere. If you'd rather the recipe apply only to a single item — say the oat milk in a cortado specifically — you can scope it to that item instead.

When a preorder is placed for a future date, does it affect inventory today?

Inventory is decremented at the moment the order fires to the kitchen — not when the order is placed.

For future orders, that generally means the future order date has to arrive, then any prep time you've configured is applied, and the order fires at the end of that. Because Toast has several settings that control fire timing — including an option not to fire until payment is received — the exact behavior depends on how your account is configured. If you're purchasing the product, work through this with your onboarding consultant.

This mostly matters if you do a meaningful volume of future or catering orders.

Do voids and refunds return stock to inventory?

It follows the standard rules of Toast:

  • If you void or cancel an order before it fires to the kitchen, there is no inventory impact at all.
  • If the order has already fired and you void it, the stock is returned and restocked. This appears in your inventory logs as a restock due to a sales void, so the reason is traceable.

If you want to refund a guest without returning stock — for example the food was made and thrown away — return the money to the payment method rather than voiding the order. Voiding is the action that restocks.

We know this distinction is confusing, and we're working on the usability of it.

How do I prevent overselling an item that's been preordered?

This isn't solved today. Because inventory decrements at fire time, preordered quantities aren't reflected in your on-hand count until the day the order fires — so you don't currently have a clear picture of what's already promised.

Committed inventory visibility is targeted for around January 2027. You'll be able to see, on the item library, how much stock you have on hand, how much is committed to future orders, and how much is in transit due to cross-location transfers. The broader concept of committed stock rolls out through 2027.

We're deliberately landing the core system first — accurate data, correct reports — before layering this kind of sophistication on top.

My menu is made-to-order or build-your-own. Will this work for me?

This is one of the areas we're focused on most, precisely because xtraChef handled it poorly. Pizza, coffee, and build-your-own are our explicit focus cohorts.

The honest answer: the more configuration and customization you offer guests, the more you have to tell us about the inventory impact of those choices. There's no way around that fundamental tradeoff. What we can do is make it far less painful to set up and maintain — and because the recipe sits on top of the modifiers you've already built, there's no mapping step and no parallel system to keep in sync.

For businesses with pre-modifiers and many modifier combinations, the modifier configuration screen will look dense. We'll provide a power user flow for that, which most customers won't need to touch.

If your base recipe is nearly empty and almost all of your cost sits in modifiers, we'd genuinely like to talk to you — reach out and we'll walk through your setup together.

Is there a default size multiplier — for example, a double using twice the liquor?

Not as a multiplier, and that's deliberate. The default multiplier in xtraChef caused more problems than it solved, so we moved away from it.

Two capabilities replace it. Size modifiers (November 2026) let each size have its own recipe with independently adjustable quantities. Shared inventory — planned, not yet built — lets you link items that draw from common stock using a ratio, without building a recipe at all. See the shared inventory question above for how that works.

Will bundles support mix and match — letting guests pick a few items from a list?

The mix-and-match capability itself — sometimes called special combos, where a guest picks one entree, one side, and one drink at a set price — is being built by a separate Toast team, and there may be a Test Kitchen you can join for it.

From the inventory side: once modifier support ships in November 2026, selections made in the checkout flow will correctly decrement the underlying inventory. So combos and inventory will work together, with the combo functionality itself on its own timeline.

Getting Started

What is Toast Inventory, and what happens to Toast Retail?

Toast Inventory is the new name for the inventory management capabilities that are part of Toast Retail today. It includes core inventory tools such as purchasing and receiving, cycle counts, a centralized catalog, and reporting that brings together sales and inventory data in a single view.

As Toast expands to serve a broader range of businesses — including restaurants, convenience stores, gas stations, retail, and hybrid operations — we're evolving our inventory solution to support more product types and operational needs. Toast Inventory provides a simple starting point for businesses just getting started with inventory management while offering the advanced capabilities needed as operations grow in complexity.

Existing Toast Retail customers will continue to have access to their inventory features, now under the Toast Inventory name. This change reflects the broader scope of the product rather than a change to the core inventory functionality customers rely on.

Rollout & Access

How do I decide whether to upgrade to Advanced Inventory?

Advanced Inventory is currently in beta and will become Toast Inventory. While it's actively being enhanced, there are a few feature gaps to consider before upgrading.

You won't be able to upgrade yet if you:

  • Use alternate packages or bundles, which are not currently supported.
  • Use Shopify, as inventory quantity synchronization between Advanced Inventory and Shopify is not yet fully supported.

You can upgrade, but should be aware that:

  • Auto 86ing is not yet available. If automatically keeping your online menu in sync with inventory levels is critical to your business, we recommend waiting until this feature is released. In the meantime, you can manually sync inventory and item availability in bulk, though it does require some manual effort.

If these limitations don't affect your operation, you're a great candidate for the beta and can take advantage of the latest inventory capabilities today.
